## What Is Happy Wheels?

Happy Wheels is a physics-based platform game created by Jim Bonacci. It is known for its unusual characters, unpredictable ragdoll physics, challenging obstacle courses, and user-created stages.

The central gameplay revolves around guiding a selected character through a course filled with moving objects, traps, gaps, ramps, and other hazards. Players must control movement, manage momentum, and react to changing conditions while trying to reach the finish.

Different characters use different forms of transportation, including bicycles, wheelchairs, and other vehicles. These differences affect how each character moves, responds to obstacles, and handles difficult sections of a level.

Happy Wheels also stands out for its level editor and community-created content. Players can explore stages designed by others and experiment with their own obstacle layouts, creating a varied experience beyond the original levels.

## Core Gameplay Features

### Physics-Based Movement

Movement is influenced by momentum, gravity, collisions, and the interaction between the character and the environment. Small changes in speed or balance can affect whether a player clears an obstacle or loses control.

### Ragdoll Physics

The game's ragdoll system creates unpredictable character reactions during collisions and falls. These exaggerated physics interactions contribute to its distinctive visual style and challenging gameplay.

### Obstacle Courses

Levels contain platforms, ramps, barriers, moving objects, and other hazards. Players must observe each section and choose suitable movements to navigate the course.

### Timing and Precision

Successful runs often depend on accelerating, braking, leaning, and jumping at the right moment. Learning the behavior of an obstacle can help players handle repeated attempts more consistently.

## Characters and Vehicles

### Different Play Styles

Each character has a distinct vehicle or movement setup. The choice affects speed, stability, maneuverability, and how the character interacts with the environment.

### Segway Guy

Segway Guy uses a self-balancing personal transporter. Players must manage movement and balance to navigate platforms and uneven terrain.

### Irresponsible Dad

Irresponsible Dad rides a bicycle with a child passenger. The vehicle's movement and balance create a different challenge from characters using other forms of transportation.

### Wheelchair Guy

Wheelchair Guy uses a powered wheelchair that handles movement differently from bicycles and other vehicles. Understanding its controls helps players navigate ramps and obstacles.

### Character Selection

Choosing a suitable character can affect the difficulty of a particular stage. Some courses are easier to approach with a vehicle that offers better stability, while others reward speed or precise movement.

## Level Design and Obstacles

### Platform Navigation

Levels often require players to cross narrow platforms, gaps, slopes, and uneven surfaces. Managing speed and balance is important when navigating these sections.

### Moving Hazards

Some courses include objects that move or activate according to specific conditions. Players must observe their timing and move through the area when an opening becomes available.

### Traps and Interactive Objects

Obstacles can include switches, barriers, launch mechanisms, and other interactive elements. Learning how these objects behave helps players understand the intended route.

### Checkpoints and Repeated Attempts

Some stages include checkpoints that reduce the need to restart from the beginning after a failed attempt. Repeating a section can help players learn its timing and improve their control.

## Physics, Momentum, and Control

### Acceleration and Braking

Controlling speed is important when approaching jumps, narrow platforms, and moving hazards. Excessive momentum can make precision difficult, while insufficient speed may prevent a character from clearing an obstacle.

### Balance and Leaning

Some vehicles require careful adjustment of the character's posture. Learning how leaning affects movement can help players recover from awkward landings and maintain stability.

### Jumps and Landings

Jumps depend on approach speed, angle, and the shape of the terrain. A controlled landing can preserve momentum and help players continue through a difficult course.

### Learning From Failed Attempts

The physics system makes experimentation a central part of the experience. Players can adjust their timing, movement, or route after observing what happened during a previous attempt.
